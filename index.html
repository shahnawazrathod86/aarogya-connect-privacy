Privacy Policy

Aarogya Connect (Android app) · Publisher: A3S DIGITECH · Last updated: 17 October 2026

In plain language: your health records, your family's profiles and the documents you keep in My Family Health stay encrypted on your phone; there is no account and no cloud copy, and A3S DIGITECH runs no server that could hold them. Location is used only when you ask for "near me" results. The app shows ads through Google AdMob. Our AI helper, AASHU, can use Google Gemini only if you switch it on, and it never sees your saved records. You can lock the app with a PIN or fingerprint, and delete everything the app stores in one tap.

1. What we store on your device

Aarogya Connect keeps your health records — blood pressure, blood glucose, vaccination history, pregnancy (ANC/PNC) checkups, TB medicine adherence, menstrual cycle entries, medication reminders, BMI calculations, daily step counts and water-intake entries, saved facilities, AYUSH daily tracker entries, saved AYUSH articles and quiz results — only on this device.

My Family Health adds more, all kept only on this device: family member profiles (name, date of birth, blood group, allergies, health conditions, notes, emergency contacts, an optional caregiver’s name and phone number), medicine schedules with the stock you enter and a log of doses you mark taken or skipped, appointments, readings you enter (blood pressure, blood sugar, weight, pulse, temperature, oxygen), the documents you add (prescriptions, lab reports, discharge summaries, bills and similar files) with their titles, tags, folders and notes, and a history of what you agreed to share. Section 3 explains how.

Every record is encrypted at rest with a 256-bit key that lives only in your device's secure keystore (Android Keystore), never inside the app's own data files. Documents are encrypted separately, file by file (section 3). The app does not allow Android or Google to back up this data (backups are switched off), so it is never copied to Google Drive or another cloud.

2. What we do not do

This app has no user account, no cloud database, and no backend server for your personal or health data. Nothing described in section 1 is ever uploaded anywhere by this app. The only things that leave your device are described in sections 5, 6 and 7 (and, only when you choose to share, section 3): ads, map and location searches, and, only if you switch it on, the questions you type to the AASHU AI helper. No analytics or tracking SDK runs in this app beyond the advertising SDK described in section 5. We do not sell health information, and we never use it to choose or target ads.

3. My Family Health: profiles, documents, reminders and the app lock

Where it lives. Everything in My Family Health is stored only on your phone. A3S DIGITECH does not run a server for it, receives none of it, and cannot see it. Because we hold no copy, we cannot recover it if your phone is lost, reset or the app is deleted, and we cannot reset a forgotten PIN. Documents you want to keep safe elsewhere can be shared from the app to yourself or to your doctor (see below).

Documents. A document you add (a photo, PDF or other file) is read straight into the app and encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it is stored. The encryption key is a separate random key kept in the Android Keystore. The encrypted file sits in the app’s private folder, which other apps cannot read. A readable copy is made only for a moment when you open a document in another app or share it, and it is erased when the app locks, when you leave the documents screen, and the next time the app starts. A document that was changed after it was saved is refused rather than opened.

Reading photos on the phone. When you add a photo of a document, the app can read its printed words using a text reader that is built into the app (Google ML Kit, bundled model). This happens entirely on your phone: the photo and the words are not uploaded, and no network request is made. The words are used only to suggest a title, date and tags, which you check and change before saving, and, if you leave “Make the words searchable” on, they are kept in the same encrypted storage so you can find a document by what it says. For this, a photo is held in a temporary file in the app’s private cache folder only while the text reader and the details form use it, and it is erased when you save or leave the form.

App lock. You can lock the app with a PIN of 4 to 8 digits and, if your phone supports it, your fingerprint or face. The PIN is never stored; only a salted one-way hash is kept in the Keystore. After five wrong tries the app makes you wait, and the wait grows with further failures. Fingerprint and face checks are done by Android itself; the app never sees your biometric data. While the lock is on, the app can hide itself in the recent-apps list and block screenshots. Protected profiles (children’s profiles start protected) ask for the PIN again before opening.

Emergency card. A person’s emergency card (name, age, blood group, allergies, health conditions, emergency contacts) can optionally be opened from the lock screen without the PIN so that someone can help in an emergency. It is off by default and you switch it on per person. Notes, medicines and documents are never shown there.

Sharing and caregiver messages. Nothing leaves the app unless you choose it. When you share a summary or a document, the app first shows exactly what will be shared and asks you to agree, then hands it to the Android share menu, and you pick the app (for example WhatsApp or email). Caregiver messages (for a missed dose or low stock) are written for you and open in your own messaging app; you read them and press send. The app never sends an SMS or any message by itself and has no SMS permission. Each agreement is written to a consent history on your phone, which you can read and clear in Privacy and security, and you can withdraw caregiver consent at any time. Once something is sent through another app, that app and the person who receives it are responsible for it; we cannot take it back.

Calendar. “Add to calendar” opens your calendar app with the appointment filled in. You save it there. The app does not ask for calendar permission and never reads your calendar.

Reminders. Medicine and appointment reminders are scheduled on your phone. The notification text stays generic (for example “You have a health reminder scheduled”) so nothing private shows on a locked screen.

Not connected to ABHA or ABDM. The app does not link to the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, ABHA numbers or any health-record network. If that is ever added it will be optional and consent-based, and this policy will be updated first.

4. Basic profile (name, sex, age, email)

If you choose to enter your name, sex, age or email address — all optional — it is stored on your device the same encrypted way as your health records, used only to personalise the app (for example greeting you by name), and never transmitted off your device by this app.

5. Advertising

Aarogya Connect shows banner ads served through Google AdMob (the Google Mobile Ads SDK). To select and measure ads, AdMob can collect your device's advertising identifier, general device information (model, OS version, language, country), coarse location derived from IP address, and ad interaction data (impressions, clicks). This data is collected and processed by Google, not stored by Aarogya Connect.

Your health data is never shared with AdMob or any advertiser. Nothing from section 1 or 3 — no health record or document, reminder or profile field — is ever passed to the ad SDK, an ad request, or any advertising partner. Ads appear only on the Home screen: never in My Family Health, on the lock screen, or beside a record or document.

You can limit ad personalisation on your device: Settings → Privacy → Ads (or Google Settings → Ads on older Android versions) lets you reset your advertising ID or opt out of personalised ads. See Google's Partner Sites policy and Privacy Policy.

6. Location and pincode search

When you search for nearby facilities, your pincode or GPS coordinates and the kind of place you searched for are sent to map-data services solely to return that one search result: Google Maps data (through the Google Gemini API, relayed by a Cloudflare service) and, when Google is unavailable, OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, Photon and Overpass APIs, plus India Post's public pincode directory. To show approximate distances, the address of each result may be looked up by your phone's own location service, which is provided by Google Play services. Only the coordinates, pincode or address needed for that search is sent; your name, health data or any other identifying information is never included. Location access is requested only when you tap "near me", never at launch, and searching by pincode is always offered as an alternative that needs no location permission.

7. AASHU, the AI helper, and Google Gemini

AASHU answers health questions in two ways, and always tells you which one wrote each answer:

  • Built-in guide — fixed, checked answers that work entirely inside the app. It sends nothing anywhere.
  • Google Gemini AI — used only if you choose "Use Google Gemini AI" after reading a clear notice. You can switch it off at any time on the AASHU screen.

When Gemini is on, the question you type and your last few chat messages are sent to Google Gemini so it can write an answer. Nothing else is sent — never your saved health records, name, age, email or location.

Please do not type personal details (names, phone numbers, medical reports) into the chat. On Google's free Gemini service, Google may use what is sent to it to improve its products. Google handles that data under Google's Privacy Policy and the Gemini API terms.

Emergency, crisis and other high-risk topics are never sent to Gemini; the app answers those itself with fixed guidance and the 108/112 emergency numbers. If Gemini is unavailable or its free limit is reached, the built-in guide answers instead. Chat messages are kept only on your screen and disappear when you leave the chat; the app does not save them.

AASHU is an AI, not a doctor. Its answers, especially those written by Gemini, can contain mistakes. They are general educational information, not a diagnosis or treatment. Always consult a qualified healthcare professional, and call 108 or 112 in an emergency.

Questions about AYUSH (Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, Sowa Rigpa, Homoeopathy) are answered only from the reviewed content in the app's AYUSH Corner and are never sent to Gemini. If nothing approved matches, AASHU says so.

Reporting an answer. The flag button under an AASHU answer opens your own email app with your question and the answer written in, so you can add a note. The app sends nothing itself; the report reaches us only if you press send in your email app.

8. Notifications

Medication, vaccination and appointment reminders are scheduled and delivered entirely on your device using Android's own notification system. No reminder content is sent through, or generated by, any external push-notification server.

9. Step counting (physical activity)

Step counting is optional. If you turn it on, Android asks for the "Physical activity" (activity recognition) permission. Aarogya Connect then reads your phone's own step counter to show today's steps, and estimates distance, calories and active minutes from those steps and, if you entered them, your height and weight. Steps and the water glasses you log are stored only on this device, in the same encrypted storage as your other health records. They are never uploaded, shared with advertisers, or sent to Google Gemini or any other service. Distance and calories are estimates, not medical measurements. You can withdraw the permission at any time in your phone's Settings; the app keeps working and you can enter steps by hand. "Delete all my data" in the app also erases your step and water history.

10. Children and family members

Aarogya Connect is a general-audience app for adults (18 and over) and is not directed at children. It lets a parent or guardian keep a child’s health information (growth, vaccination, medicines, appointments, documents) and lets an adult keep records for family members they look after, such as an elderly parent.

Before a child is added, the parent or legal guardian confirms in the app that they are the child’s parent or guardian and agree to the child’s information being kept in the app on that phone. For another adult, the person adding them confirms that the adult knows and agrees. Each confirmation is recorded in the consent history on the phone. A child’s profile is protected by default (it asks for the PIN before opening), the child’s information is never used for ads or tracking, and, like all other data, it stays on the phone and is never sent to us. A3S DIGITECH does not collect, monitor or profile any person’s information, including a child’s.

11. Your control over your data

You can review what is stored, and permanently delete all of it, at any time from Profile → My data → Delete all my data. Deletion is immediate and on the device: every health record, reminder and profile field is wiped, scheduled reminders are cancelled, and the app reopens as if freshly installed. We cannot undo this because we never had a copy. It also erases every encrypted document, the app lock PIN and its settings, and every family profile. You can remove just one person (and everything kept for them, documents included) from their profile, delete a single document or medicine, clear the consent history, and switch Google Gemini off at any time on the AASHU screen.

Your rights. Because we hold none of your data, you can already see, correct, copy and erase it yourself inside the app. If you have a question, a complaint or want to exercise a right under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, write to a3sdigitech@gmail.com and we will reply within 30 days.

Your copy of your data. Profile → Privacy & data → Export my data copies everything the app stores (your health records, reminders, profile, family members, saved items and tracker entries) to your phone's clipboard as plain, readable text; My Family Health → a person → Share and caregiver → Copy this person's records does the same for one person. Documents are not copied this way: open a document and use Share to send it to yourself or your doctor. The app does not send, save or upload that copy anywhere; what you do with it next is up to you. The copy is not encrypted, so paste it only somewhere private, such as a note only you can open, and delete it when you no longer need it. Other apps on your phone may be able to read the clipboard while it holds the copy; Android shows a notice when an app does so.

13. Medical disclaimer

Aarogya Connect provides general educational information and personal record-keeping tools. It is not a diagnosis or treatment and does not replace a qualified healthcare professional. In an emergency, call your local emergency number (108 or 112 in India).

14. Where this app is offered

Aarogya Connect is made for people in India. Its hospitals, government schemes (such as PM-JAY), emergency numbers (108 and 112) and languages are Indian, and it is offered on Google Play in India only.
